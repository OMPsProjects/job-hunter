# Humanizer rules for resumes

Resumes read as machine-written for a few recurring reasons. `python job_hunter.py lint`
catches the mechanical ones. These rules cover the rest; apply them by reading every line.

## Words

Never: spearheaded, leveraged, robust, seamless, dynamic, passionate, results-driven,
proven track record, cutting-edge, synergy, fostered, navigated, pivotal, delve, tapestry,
landscape, ensuring, showcasing, underscoring, utilize, orchestrated, championed, empowered,
elevate, embark, realm, resonate, harness, unlock, meticulous, holistic, transformative,
unwavering, testament, fast-paced, world-class, best-in-class, thought leader, detail-oriented,
team player, self-starter, responsible for, duties included, various, innovative, seasoned.

Use plain verbs a person in the field would use: ran, built, wrote, sized, priced, closed,
managed, reviewed, cut, opened, hired, trained, fixed, sold, negotiated, led.

## Shape

- No em dashes. Use a comma, colon, or a new sentence. Date ranges use a hyphen.
- No exclamation marks, no "I", no "not only ... but also".
- Don't end bullets with an "-ing" tail ("..., ensuring compliance", "..., driving growth").
  State the result as its own clause or cut it.
- Don't stack three abstract nouns ("efficiency, scalability, and growth"). Name the thing.
- Vary how bullets start. No more than two bullets in a role starting with the same verb.
- Mix bullet lengths. Most 10-25 words; a few short ones are fine.
- Specifics beat adjectives: asset type, deal size, count, system, team size, time frame.
  If there's no number in the source, don't invent one; describe the work concretely instead.

## Summary paragraph

Two or three sentences, written like the person would say it to a hiring manager. Lead with
what they do and for how long, then what they're best at, then (optional) what they want next.
No "seeking a challenging opportunity", no list of adjectives.

## Final read

Read it aloud in your head as the person. Anything they wouldn't say in an interview comes out.
